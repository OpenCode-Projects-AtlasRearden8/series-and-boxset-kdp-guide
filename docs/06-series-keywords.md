# Per-book vs omnibus keywords

Every KDP product gets its own seven keyword slots, and the single most
common series mistake is pasting the *same* seven phrases into *every*
book. Done that way, your own titles compete with each other for the
same few searches, and the omnibus still has no keywords of its own.
This chapter is the split: which intents belong on each individual book,
and which belong only on the bundle.

(For the craft of *writing* good slots — scoring, clustering, risky-
word flags — use **book-keyword-lab-cli**; this chapter covers only the
series-level split.)

## What the slots are for

Keyword slots in KDP feed store search and category placement. Three
rules that matter at series scale:

- **Slots are per-product, so seven slots × five books = 35 slots** —
  but only if you use them differently. Identical slots across books
  are the same 7 used five times.
- **The series name field is separate** from keywords. Don't spend a
  keyword slot on your series name; it's already on every book.
- **Amazon-style keyword slots ignore bare digits and repetitive
  punctuation** (historical store behavior — verify current guidance):
  write "complete series box set" rather than relying on "1 2 3" to do
  work.

## Per-book slots: reader-intent keywords

Each book should carry the **genre + subgenre + trope** long-tails for
its own story — the searches a *book-level* buyer would type:

| Slot theme | Example phrasing (fiction) | Example phrasing (non-fiction) |
|------------|----------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Genre + subgenre | small town cozy mystery | AI guide for beginners |
| Trope / hook | grumpy sheriff small town romance | no jargon step by step |
| Series-adjacent | cozy culinary mystery series | practical AI workbook series |
| Setting/flavor | lakeside summer murder mystery | local tools offline guide |
| Reader-identity | books for busy nurses | tools for small business owners |
| Mood/content note | heartwarming clean whodunit | plain english friendly guide |
| Comparison/taste | like a Thursday night mystery | like a trusted friend explains |

Note the pattern: a **series-word phrase** ("cozy culinary mystery
series") earns a slot on each book because that's how readers *browse a
genre series* — but "complete series" does **not** belong on singles.

## Omnibus slots: bundle-intent keywords

The bundle is a different product with a different buyer — often
someone who already knows they want several books at once. Its slots
should say so (and its description should too):

| Slot theme | Example phrasing |
|------------|------------------|
| Bundle shape | complete series |
| Format word | box set |
| Collection word | omnibus edition |
| Genre + bundle | cozy mystery series box set |
| Value story | three books one guide |
| Gift intent | binge read collection |
| Continuation | books in order |

The genre and trope phrases still earn a slot or two on the bundle (a
fresh reader may find the *bundle* first), but the **majority** of the
omnibus's seven slots should be bundle-words, because that's searching
no single book can serve.

## What never to duplicate across products

- **"Complete series" / "box set"** on singles: confusing (a single
  book *isn't* a complete series) and it wastes a slot that could carry
  a trope.
- **The identical seven slots** on every book: compete-with-yourself.
- **Brand names, other authors' series names, your own product** terms
  like "Kindle" in keyword slots: against KDP listing rules and a
  fast way to get a listing blocked.
- **Extremes ("best", "free")**: also listing-rule problems, not
  keyword problems.

## Categories, in one paragraph

Categories in KDP are chosen at upload (three picks) and can be shifted
later. For a series it works like keywords: put the singles in the
*book* categories for the genre, and if the store offers a box-set or
collection category in your genre (it often does), the bundle gets
that. The store will also auto-list the bundle near its singles because
of the shared series fields — chapter 1's work quietly paying off.

## A tiny audit ritual

Once a year (or when a book's ranking stops making sense):

1. List every product's current seven slots in your series tracker.
2. Flag any phrase that appears identically on two products.
3. For each single, ask: "does every slot still describe *this* book?"
4. For the bundle, ask: "would a stranger know this is several books?"

Then fix, republish metadata, and let the ripple settle for a week
before judging.
