# Pricing ladders

A series is not five books at five independent prices; it is one
**ladder** a reader climbs, and you choose where each rung sits. This
chapter sets up the classic ladder, then does the e-book band arithmetic
that decides where the boxset belongs — including the surprising
arithmetic that **$9.99 can earn a bundle more than $14.99 does**.
(Round numbers; the exact per-unit math is kdp-profit-planner's job.)

## The e-book royalty bands (Amazon's US rates, round numbers)

KDP e-books pay either **35%** or **70%**, and the switch is your list
price:

| List price | Royalty rate | Example earnings |
|-----------:|-------------:|-----------------:|
| $0.99      | 35%          | ~$0.35           |
| $1.99      | 35%          | ~$0.70           |
| $2.99      | 70%          | ~$2.09           |
| $3.99      | 70%          | ~$2.79           |
| $4.99      | 70%          | ~$3.49           |
| $5.99      | 70%          | ~$4.19           |
| $6.99      | 70%          | ~$4.89           |
| $7.99      | 70%          | ~$5.59           |
| $8.99      | 70%          | ~$6.29           |
| $9.99      | 70%          | ~$6.99           |
| $10.00+    | 35%          | varies           |

(Rates differ slightly by marketplace; on the US store the 70% band runs
**$2.99–$9.99**. Verify per-marketplace in KDP before you set a price.)

The two cliffs to remember:

- **Below $2.99** the royalty *rate* drops to 35% — a penny's move from
  $2.99 down to $2.98 nearly *halves* your per-unit pay ($2.09 → $1.04),
  which is the same logic run in reverse when you *price up* to $2.99:
  the band boundary is worth a whole rung of thinking.
- **Above $9.99** the rate drops: $10.00 pays $3.50 — *less* than
  $9.99's $6.99. E-books priced between about **$10 and $19.97** earn
  strictly worse per unit than $9.99.

## The ladder, shape first

For most 3–6-book fiction series the working shape is:

| Rung | Product | Typical list | Purpose |
|------|---------|-------------:|---------|
| 1 | Book 1 (the "door") | $0.99–$2.99 | cheap to try; the promo rung |
| 2 | Books 2..N | $3.99–$5.99 | the steady-income rungs |
| 3 | E-book bundle | $9.99 | the "get everything" rung |
| 4 | Print singles | $9.99–$12.99 | slower, but real |
| 5 | Print bundle | $19.99–$24.99 | the high-ticket rung |

The ladder gives every kind of reader a rung that fits — the browser,
the commuter, the binge reader in Kindle Unlimited, the "I only buy hard
covers" relative — and it gives you exact places to run promos from
(chapter 7).

## The boxset rung: why $9.99 usually beats $14.99

A bundle "should" cost less than the sum of its singles — that's what
makes it bundle-shaped. So the natural instinct is: three singles at
$4.99 sum to $14.97, so bundle at $14.99 sounds "cheap by a penny".

But watch the band math:

```text
$14.99 bundle → 35% band → about   $5.25 per sale
$ 9.99 bundle → 70% band → about   $6.99 per sale
```

**The $9.99 bundle pays about $1.74 more per copy** — and it costs the
reader almost five dollars less. That's a rare case where everyone
involved wins; genuinely one of the few royalty-band loopholes worth
remembering.

The band also says when to ignore it:

```text
35% band catches up to $6.99 when price × 0.35 ≥ $6.99 → about $20
```

So: a **jumbo omnibus** (five or more books, or 400k+ words) priced
around $20–$25 earns more per copy than a $9.99 rung ever could:

| Bundle list | Royalty rate | Earnings |
|------------:|-------------:|---------:|
| $9.99       | 70%          | ~$6.99   |
| $14.99      | 35%          | ~$5.25   |
| $19.99      | 35%          | ~$7.00   |
| $24.99      | 35%          | ~$8.75   |

In terms of the singles' sums: **the $9.99 rung is the price of about
two $4.99 singles; the ~$20 rung is the price of about four.** The
in-between prices ($10–$19.97) are the dead zone — price above it, or
below it.

## The print rungs

Print has no 70%/35% cliff — print pays `60% of (list − print cost)` at
every list price — so print ladders are about **cost floors**, not
bands. Rules of thumb for a ~800-page bundle (about $10.45 print cost —
chapter 3):

- Single print books: comfortably **$9.99–$12.99** depending on length.
- Print bundle: the bundle's *floor* is print cost (≈ $10.45); a useful
  rung is **two singles' worth** ($19.99, pays ~$5.72) or **two-plus
  singles' worth** ($24.99, pays ~$8.72).
- Bundles older than about 24 months: consider a modest list drop only
  if the print cost has changed or returned stock justifies it — print
  prices don't do the "band dance" e-books do.

## Ladder maintenance

1. **Before a new launch** (chapter 7), nudge rungs: book 1 can dip;
   books 2..N stay; the bundle holds at $9.99 or your chosen tier.
2. **After a promo week**, let prices settle for at least a clean week
   before judging results — a price change takes a day or few to ripple
   through stores.
3. **One ladder per format family.** The e-book ladder and the print
   ladder move independently; don't "fix" one by dragging the other.
4. **The bundle rung is not a clearance bin.** Bundles earn their
   discount by containing several books you refuse to pretend are
   worth less.
