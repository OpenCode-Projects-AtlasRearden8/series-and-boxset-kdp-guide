# Wide vs KU round numbers

Does this series go into **Kindle Unlimited** (Amazon-exclusive e-book,
paid in page reads) or run **wide** (Amazon at standard rates, plus
every other store)? There is no single right answer — but there is a
five-line rubric here, and round-number arithmetic that makes the
trade-off visible.

(The *channel setup* — aggregators, direct stores, libraries, ISBNs —
is the territory of **publishing-beyond-kdp-guide**. This chapter only
rounds off the series-level *decision*.)

## The two economics, stated plainly

**KU path.** Your e-books earn a per-page payout on pages KU members
actually turn (KENP). Payout varies month to month, roughly
**$0.0040–$0.0045 a page — call it 0.4¢**:

| Fully read product | Pages | ≈ KU earnings |
|--------------------|------:|--------------:|
| A 300-page novel   | 300   | ~$1.25        |
| A 600-page novel   | 600   | ~$2.50        |
| A 3-book bundle (900 pages) | 900 | ~$3.75 |
| A 5-book binge (1,500 pages) | 1,500 | ~$6.25 |

In KU, a big bundle is your best binge machine: one borrow can flip
900–1,500 pages, which pays like 1–2 full-price sales — and the reader
paid nothing extra, so read-through friction inside KU is near zero.
Readers binge *because* it's prepaid.

**Wide path.** Each sale pays at list-price rates — on Amazon about
**70% in the $2.99–$9.99 band** ($3.49 on a $4.99 single), on other
stores roughly 60–70%-ish of the list depending on channel, on a direct
store 85%+ (publishing-beyond-kdp-guide, chapter 6). Wide earns per
*sale*, and consistently across readers who leave the top store.

## The round-number showdown

Per book-1 *sale* in each world, for a healthy 4-book fiction series
(65% / 75% read-through, $4.99 singles):

| What one book-1 buyer generates | KU path | Wide-ish path |
|--------------------------------|--------:|--------------:|
| Book 1 | ~$1.25 (fully read) | ~$3.49 (Amazon) |
| Books 2–4 (read-through) | ~$3.75 (3 novels read) | ~$8.76 (65% → 49% → 37% ladder × $3.49) |
| If they buy the bundle instead of singles | ~$3.75 (900-page binge) | ~$6.99 |
| **Per buyer total** | **~$5.00** | **~$12.25** |

Your mileage genuinely varies, and the *shape* of the comparison
matters more than the totals:

- **Wide pays more per fully-engaged reader** — per-reader margin is
  simply bigger at sale prices, and direct sales pay the best of all.
- **KU collects from readers who would never buy** — a slice of
  Amazon-only subscribers who buy nothing but get 1,500 binge pages
  free of your marketing. On binge-shaped genres that slice is real.
- **KU costs you the wide slice** — its 90-day-per-title exclusivity
  (renewable) takes the same books off every other e-book store for
  that stretch.

## The five-line rubric

1. **Fiction series readers binge (romance, mystery, fantasy, litRPG,
   cozy, sports...)?** KU earns from the bingers; consider it seriously.
2. **Non-fiction or problem-to-solution series?** Readers buy the one
   book that answers them; page reads stay low — lean wide + direct.
3. **You have a newsletter/audience you can sell to directly?** Wide +
   direct store; your email list is a store you own.
4. **Series slower than one release per ~3 months?** KU's binge
   audience decays between releases; wide titles just keep sitting on
   the shelf earning.
5. **Mixed setup?** Totally allowed: singles wide, bundle in KU (one
   borrow covers three books!), or one series in KU while a finicky
   series stays wide. Enrollment is per-product, decided every 90 days.

Each of these is a *testable* bet: enroll one 90-day stretch, watch the
KENP totals against the wide-margin numbers above, then decide with
your series' own data in
[kdp-sales-tracker-webapp](https://github.com/dylanasdale).

## Where the numbers get fuzzy on purpose

Page-read payouts shift every month, and the test is always *your*
read-through rate × *your* payout month. The rubric above survives
rounding: fuzzier than any monthly change, but much more stable than
the choice itself.
