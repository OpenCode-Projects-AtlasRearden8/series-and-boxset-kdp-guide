# Launch calendar and 99-cent math

You don't launch a book into a series; you add the book to a plan that
already says where every other book will be, at what price, and when.
This chapter is that plan: a week-by-week calendar to adapt, and the
compact arithmetic of the tried-and-true **99-cent launch week** that
demonstrates the point.

## The shape before the calendar

Two facts drive the schedule:

1. **Series launch with more than one book whenever you can.** The
   cheapest way to buy read-through (chapter 5) is to have book 2
   exist. If your drafting speed allows two or three releases inside
   eight weeks, schedule that before any promotion.
2. **The 99-cent week runs after the follow-through is set.** A dipping
   book 1 on a launch day with no book 2 wastes its week. The classic
   pattern is: books 1 and 2 launch close together, book 3 launches on
   a schedule, and the book-1 dip runs in book 3's launch week, when
   there are three rungs to climb.

## The 99-cent week arithmetic, in one box

Full numbers live in chapter 5; here's the pocket version:

```text
book 1 at $0.99 during promo  → you earn about $0.35 per copy
book 1 at $4.99 at full price → you earn about $3.49 per copy

cost of the dip     ≈ $3.15 of margin per promo sale
                      (the reader also saw book 1 five dollars cheaper)
pay-back per reader ≈ book 2: 65% × $3.49 = $2.27
                      book 3: 75% × 65% × $3.49 ≈ $1.70
                      total ≈ $3.97
```

Per reader, the pay-back (~$3.97 of downstream margin from ~$3.15 of
discount given per promo sale) is **positive but not enormous**. The
week becomes obviously worth it when either the volume lift is big
(promo newsletters stacking) or read-through is great. The worksheet
(W1) has the fill-in boxes with *your* rates and prices; the plan below
is where those readers come from.

(Chapter 5 carries the full four-book version — bundle rung included,
with a ~1.7× pay-back verdict. This pocket version is deliberately
shorter.)

## The week-by-week launch calendar

A rhythm to adapt — for a second series book (the first happens once):

| When | What happens | Money decision in play |
|------|--------------|------------------------|
| Week −14 | series name/numbers verified (chapter 1); preorders open on the new book | preorder ladder set *before* launch week |
| Week −10 | interior and cover done; bundle/trim math re-checked (chapter 3) if the book joins an existing print bundle family |
| Week −6 | ladder check: every other rung's price observed for a clean week before launch |
| Week −4 | newsletter/audience heads-up; "series page" tour in your notes to readers |
| Week −2 | review team and ARC reader copies go out (use your ARC tracker, not a hopeful spreadsheet) |
| Week 0 | launch at full price; back matter of *all* prior books updated to point to the new title |
| Week +1 | **99-cent week on book 1** (or on your chosen door rung) — stack promo newsletters and personal hype here, not in week 0 |
| Week +2 | prices rest for an interval; watch read-through in units |
| Week +3 | ladder maintenance: door rung returns to its rest price ($2.99–$3.99 for most) |
| Week +6–12 | the *occasional* bundle-discount week or print-bundle appearance |

### Why the dip comes *after* the full-price launch

Store algorithms and honest-to-goodness economics both like a
book's first week at its real rung: readers who wanted it pay full
price, and the launch's natural earnings aren't discounted away. The
dip week is your *acquisition* tool aimed at new-to-you readers; the
launch week is your existing-audience week. Running both at once gives
away margin to readers who should have paid — and who still would have
bought mid-promo.

## Launch-calendar arithmetic to keep on one line

Three round numbers the calendar rests on:

- **$0.35**: per-copy earnings during a $0.99 promo week (35% band).
- **≈ 6×**: the volume lift a *successful* public promo week usually
  takes to pay for the dip (chapter 5's worked example needs 600 units
  on a 100/month baseline to break even-plus).
- **1–3 days**: how long a price change takes to ripple through stores —
  schedule a dip's start and end date *at least three days* inside the
  week you intend, then confirm the live price each morning of promo
  week.

## The fifty-cent version

$0.99 is not mandatory; the arithmetic scales directly. A door at $1.99
still pays 35% (≈ $0.70), the dip costs the reader less psychology
against the $4.99 rung, and read-through pays back ~$3.97 of sequels the same
way — many authors run "half-price door week" precisely this way. Keep
the ladder logic, choose the price with W1's boxes. Numbers may drift a
hair with rounding ($3.97 vs $3.98) — that's fine; the *decision window*
is wide.
